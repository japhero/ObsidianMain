### Existence and uniqueness theorem for nonlinear systems

- remember [[Areas/school/Math/Elementary Differential Equations with Boundary Value Problems/Chapter 2#Existence and Uniqueness|exsistence and uniqueness theorem in chapter 2]]

> [!definition] **Theorem**
> Consider the (n+1)-dimensional open rectangle region R defined by $a<t<b,\alpha_{1}<y_{1}<\boldsymbol{\beta_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{n}<y_{n}<\beta_{n}$. Assume that the initial condition $(t_{0},y_{0})$ lies in the region $R$. Let $f(t,y)$ and the partial derivatives 
> $$
> \frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial y_{1}}, \frac{\partial f_{1}}{y_{2}},\dots \frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial y_{n}},
> \frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial y_{1}}
> \frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial y_{2}}, \dots
> \frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial y_{n}},\dots 
> \frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial y_{1}},
> \frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial y_{2}}, \dots
> \frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial y_{n}}
> $$ 
> be continuous in R. Then the initial value problem has a unique solution $\mathbf{y(t)}$ that exists on some t-interval (c,d) containing $t_{0}$.
> 
> 


- If the functions inside of the initial value problems are not dependent on t then we call the function and autonomous function.
- Equilibrium solution is when the solution becomes zero.

### Autonomous and general forms/stability
- Autonomous equations are of the form 
$$
y'=f(y)
$$
- Stability means that given a small change in its position it returns to equilibrium. 
- Instability means that given a small shift the point starts moving in an "uncontrolled manner".


### Closeness

- given two vectors $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ we can the distance between those to vectors with $\vec{u}-\vec{v}$. lets say
$$
\begin{align}
\vec{u}= \begin{bmatrix}
u_{1} \\
u_{2} \\
\vdots \\
u_{n}
\end{bmatrix}, & & v= 
\begin{bmatrix}
v_{1} \\
v_{2} \\
\vdots \\
v_{n}
\end{bmatrix}

\end{align}
$$
We can find the norm of the two vectors with 

$$
||u-v || = \sqrt{ (u_{1}-v_{1})^2+(u_{2}-v_{2})^2 +\dots+(u_{n}-v_{n})^2}
$$


### Definition for stability
- in general we dont say that a system is stable or unstable but rather we mention the specific equilbirum point at which it is either stable or unstable



**Def**
we say that $y_{e}$ is stable if given any $\epsilon>0$ there exsists $a \,\zeta>0$ such that if $|| y_{0}-y_{e} || < \zeta,$ then $||y(t)-y_{c}|| <\epsilon$ for all $t>0$. If a system is not stable at an equilibrium point we call it unstable.
