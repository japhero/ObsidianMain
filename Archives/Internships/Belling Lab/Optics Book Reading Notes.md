## Terms Reference
 
- $\mathrm{\eta_{ext}}$ is the **external quantum efficiency** and is how much the photodiode can create a current from the incoming light/photons 
- (Responsivity) $\mathrm{R_{pd}}=\frac{I_{pd}}{P_{opt}}$ where $\mathrm{P_{opt}}$ is the optical input power 
	- This is measure of performance because it tells us the amount of current generated for a given amount of optical input power. 
- (Carrier transit time) the time it takes a photogenerated electron or hole to travel through the active region before being collected by the contacts.  
- Thermal noise/Johnson noise is the noise due to thermal motion of the electros in conductors and is generated in all resistances of the photodiode. 
- p-type material has an abundance of holes 
	- P because it contains positively charged holes 
- n-type material has an abundance of electrons 
	- N because the electrons it contains have a negative charge
- **BER** or the bit error rate is the probability of false identification of a bit by the decision circuit in the receiver. 
- $\mathrm{P_{rec}}$ is the average optical power that is necessary to achieve a error free detection at a given bitrate. 




# 6.2 Photodiode Types


### P-I-N Photodiode 
- Consists of the p type then an <u>intrinsic absorber</u> and then another highly doped n type material 
	- Close to normal diode
	- This gives lower junction capacitance and more freedom in designing the thickness of the depleted high field region.

### 6.2.3 Avalanche Photodiodes
- Achieves high sensitivity due to internal gain 
- Photocarriers are accelerated in a high field drift region to such an extent that they generate new electron hole pairs by impact ionization. 
	- A single photon can produce multiple electron hole pairs 
- They provide higher sensitivity due to the gain
